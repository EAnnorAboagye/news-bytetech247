Apple has trained its own large language model specifically for the Chinese market with technical
support from Alibaba, according to a Reuters report citing people familiar with the matter,
marking what Reuters describes as the first such approval for a foreign firm's proprietary AI
model by Beijing.

## What Reuters reported

The self-trained model will run alongside third-party systems already licensed for China, most
notably Alibaba's Qwen, in what multiple outlets covering the Reuters report describe as a
"dual-track" approach, according to
[coverage from MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/08/14/apple-trained-own-ai-model-for-china/)
and corroborating reports from Yahoo Finance and TechRepublic. Baidu's technology is also expected
to play a role in the China version of Apple Intelligence. China's Cyberspace Administration
registered Apple's generative AI service the prior month, clearing a regulatory hurdle after a
lengthy review, per the same reporting.

## Why Apple needed this

Chinese iPhone buyers have increasingly favored domestic brands with built-in AI assistants, and
the absence of advanced AI features on Chinese iPhones has been cited as a factor weighing on
Apple's sales there, the reporting says. Training its own model, rather than relying entirely on
third-party Chinese models, gives Apple more direct control over the AI experience across iPhone,
iPad, Mac, and Vision Pro in the market. Neither Apple nor Alibaba has issued its own public
statement on the arrangement; this account rests on Reuters' sourcing as relayed by the outlets
above.

## Why it matters

A foreign company training and operating its own AI model inside China's regulatory system, rather
than licensing a domestic model wholesale, is a meaningfully different arrangement than what other
foreign tech firms have managed to get approved there, and it suggests Apple judged the China AI
feature gap serious enough to justify the unusual regulatory lift. The dual-track structure, an
Apple-trained model running alongside Alibaba's Qwen, looks like a hedge: it gives Apple a
proprietary asset in the market without fully cutting ties to the Alibaba partnership that likely
helped the approval happen in the first place. Whether this actually closes the AI-feature gap
with domestic competitors, or just narrows it, will depend on capabilities Reuters' reporting
doesn't yet detail.

What to watch: an on-the-record confirmation or product announcement from Apple, and whether
China-specific Apple Intelligence features actually ship with this model once approved.
